# Design: talking, evidence, Psychic and the confrontation

Agreed with the designer, October 2026. **Built in v1.11–v1.17**, one step per version (see "Building it"). The
few parts not built yet are listed at the end and in FOLLOWUP.md. Where this file and the code disagree, say so and
decide which one wins; don't let them drift.

## Why

The game was too hard, too confusing, and didn't give the player enough freedom. Most of its rules were hidden
bookkeeping: a crowd "lean" built from ten modifiers, cards graded clear or fuzzy by the hour and by THEIR day,
daily limits on verbs, and a Psychic bar that locked you out of talking. Evidence felt like filing, not like play.

The test for every system: it should fit in one sentence a player could say out loud, with no "unless", "only when"
or "except". Where a rule needs one, cut the exception.

## What stays

- The five-day week, the town, the cast, the three factions.
- THEIR awareness of you is settled at 06:00. It must never move the moment someone notices you, because that
  would tell you who THEY are.
- Four frames on every suspect, and the evidence book.
- Cards explain what they show. Those texts are part of the fun.
- Fights, hunts, traps, the FBI, being taken, Friday.

## Talking

- **Press is gone.** Gossip covers it.
- **No daily limits** on any verb. Asking again just gets "I've told you everything."
- **Gossip is a clue, never evidence.** It never makes a card. What you hear goes on the board as a note under the
  face it's about. It's built from what people really saw in town, and it often points at the wrong person: people
  talk about whoever looks odd, and the people THEY got to lie.
- **What Press used to find moves into gossip:** someone who saw a change tells you about it, a bought person
  praises whoever paid them, a victim says they don't remember last night. All of it is clues.
- **Chat** calms someone who finds you weird. The person you chat with can also tell you facts about themselves
  (see Biography), and one of THEM can let an odd fact slip without noticing.
- **No friends and no enemies.** Liking goes. The only thing people hold about you is how weird they find you
  (see Suspicion).

## Evidence

### Four frames

| Frame | What it is | How you get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Home** | A thing found at their place | You take it |
| **Habit** | Something a person could do, done wrong | You photograph it |
| **Body** | The body doing something a body can't | You photograph it |
| **Biography** | An odd fact about their life | You pick it in the evidence book |

Contact is gone. Shapes are Body, behaviour is Habit, things are Home.

There is no clear or fuzzy any more. A card says plainly what it shows, where and when ("Priya · tongue, too long ·
supply closet"), and the player judges it. Whether a card holds up is only decided in a confrontation: ✓ or ✗.

### The rule that holds it together

**A human never has more than two odd true things about them**: one innocent oddity, plus at most one thing THEY
planted on them. One of THEM can have four. So three ✓ in a confrontation always means THEM, and two never prove
anything.

### Things and photos

**You take things. You photograph people.**

- Taking a thing puts it in your pocket. If it was at someone's place, it also goes onto their board under Home,
  automatically. The card shows a small picture of where it was found and two words: "Earl's desk", "Pell's back
  room".
- That can put a card on an innocent, for example a listening device in Joanne's lamp. That's fine; see the rule
  above.
- A thing with no owner (in the street, or in a shared place like the office microwave) isn't evidence. It's a clue
  ("who was just in here?") and trap bait. THEY come back for things hidden in shared places, and a photo of whoever
  does is a Habit card.
- Taking has a cost: the owner notices it's gone, THEY can come to your flat to take it back, and it can bait a
  trap.
- Photographing a thing gives a plain print and the hint "Take it to use as evidence".
- A photo of a true form with no face to it is a scary photo, not a card.
- Cards are never used up. Only THEY can take one, from your flat.

### Home

"Their place" is their desk, their home, or the shop's back storage for the trader. Every suspect has one.

Every hidden thing is one of four kinds:

1. **Junk.** Most finds: a receipt, an odd sock, a broken watch. It makes searching feel real.
2. **Human lookalikes.** Innocent secrets that look like THEIRS.
3. **THEIR own things.** The owner is one of THEM.
4. **THEIR plant.** THEY got to this person (bought, bitten or being probed), and the owner is human. Never in
   the first few days.

Things are coloured by their faction's look (scales and gold; chrome and green light; rot and old newspaper), never
by what they mean, so a plant looks just as alien as THEIR own things.

No frame-ups: THEY never plant THEIR own things on an innocent to frame them. The plant is the trick instead.

| | THEIR own things (it's them) | Human lookalikes | The fridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lizards** | Shed skin in the laundry, brass tokens stamped with scales, a cooler of eaten fish (take it and you stink). THEY hide a lot of fish | A fisherman's bucket of trout, a sunlamp | Nothing but fish |
| **Aliens** | A laser pistol, a humming cylinder, a star chart with Wexley circled | A ham radio | Empty and spotless |
| **The dead** | Their own obituary, a jar of teeth, a finger in newspaper | A butcher's big order | Raw meat, going off |

- The fridge is one more place to search. A human's fridge can look odd too: a broke person's is empty, someone
  who loves fish has a lot of it.
- The lookalikes already in the game stay: a cash envelope (a gambling debt), heart pills, love letters, a
  Halloween mask, a police scanner.

### THEIR plant

One object per faction. It means the same thing wherever it's found.

| | The plant | At a human's place | In your home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aliens** | A listening device | They're being watched, and may be taken soon | THEY listen to you: THEY learn about you faster |
| **Lizards** | Stolen cash from Pell's till, scales on the band | Their payment: they're bought | Planted to frame you: the police (bought from day 2) raid you |
| **The dead** | A mark scratched on the door frame | They've been bitten | You're marked: THEY come for you that night |

- In **your** home you can **Destroy** it.
- At a **victim's** place there is no Destroy, because offering it would give away that the thing isn't evidence.
  **Taking it** as evidence protects them instead: none of THEM comes for that person that night. The exception is
  THEIR nights 4 and 5, when hell is let loose.

### Signs for gut feeling

Not cards and not photos. They put someone on your radar, and watching that person can lead you to THEM.

| THEY got to them by | What you might notice |
| --- | --- |
| A bite (the dead) | Stays home ill the day after; their desk is empty |
| Abduction (aliens) | Comes to work after lunch, dazed: they slept the morning off |
| A bribe (lizards) | Money: more trips to the shop, something new at home (a new PC, gold art) |

### Habit

Something a person could do, done wrong. Only a photo counts; just seeing it adds a note to the board.

| | THEIR habit | Human lookalike |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lizards** | Hugs the radiator, eyes shut; eats raw fish at lunch | Someone with a cold by the radiator; a fisherman's sushi |
| **Aliens** | Clicks at the wall or the server rack; takes a phone apart and stares at it | Martin from IT fixing the server rack: it's his job |
| **The dead** | Eats raw meat from the fridge with bare hands; stands facing a corner for minutes | Someone on a raw-food diet; someone asleep on their feet after a night shift |

Also Habit: handing someone an envelope, fetching a hidden thing, and THEIR gathering at night (as today; it is not
a frame of its own). "Swaying in the corner" moves here from Body.

**The slipping-off cue.** Anyone about to slip off shows it: a look over the shoulder, a glance at the watch, then
they get up. Everyone does it (humans before their own secret, THEM before THEIR business), so the cue gives nobody
away. You follow, stay out of sight, and take the photo at the spot.

### Body

The shape slipping, caught on film:

- Lizards: the tongue, peeling skin.
- Aliens: floating with lit eyes, the face slipping.
- The dead: an arm coming off.
- Any faction: the tenth forms, the half change, the full change.

It's hard to catch and dangerous, and that's fine. Two things keep it fair:

- **You can hear it.** Every slip makes a sound you can hear through a wall from close by. (Today the first few are
  skipped on purpose; that goes.) Hear it, sneak up, peek through a door left ajar, take the photo.
- **The flash rule.** Within about 3 steps, from any side, they notice the photo. Further away and not in front of
  them, they don't. When they notice, "!" appears over their head.

### Biography

Every suspect has a page of about six facts in the evidence book. Facts you haven't found yet show as blank lines.

- **Where facts come from:** chatting with the person themselves, rummaging at their place, the newspaper, Mom's
  calls. Not gossip: gossip stays clues only.
- **Facts are always true.** What they mean is up to you.
- **The card:** you mark one fact per person as odd, and that fact is their Biography card. You can move the mark
  until you confront them.
- **In a confrontation:** a fact that really is odd gets ✓; an ordinary one gets ✗ ("So? Lots of people moved here
  in March.").
- A human can have one odd-looking fact. It counts toward their two.

| | What's odd about THEIR facts |
| --- | --- |
| **Lizards** | They don't live long, so nothing is old. Instead, a royal bloodline, or they turn up at elite things (the country club, the governor's ball) while not looking elite at all |
| **Aliens** | Their data is faked, so their facts don't agree with each other: born in Ohio on one paper, in Toronto on another. Don't overdo it: most of their facts look normal |
| **The dead** | Their data is very old: "I was friends with the man who invented the radio." They can say it themselves, without noticing |

## The confrontation

It replaces the arena: the testimony, the crowd's lean, rebuttals, "How well do you know me?", the counterattack
and the escape bar all go.

1. Face someone and pick **Confront**. With nobody around, it's Attack, the private fight.
2. Time stops. The camera swings round to frame you, IT and everyone listening, lit like a stage where you stand.
3. Your case is every card on their board; there's no choosing. The cards fan out over IT one by one, and each flips
   to ✓ or ✗ with one line from IT: "Yes, I like fish. So?" or "That's not mine. Never seen it."
4. The outcome plays out in the street.

**✓** means the card really is about them (for Biography: the fact you picked really is odd). **✗** means it isn't.
That's mostly a thing THEY dropped at someone else's place: a skin in Joanne's hallway after a night attack lands on
Joanne. A ✗ simply doesn't count.

| ✓ | What happens (the same for anyone) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 or 1 | They shrug it off, and everyone listening now thinks you're mad |
| 2 | It turns ugly: they run or hit you. One of THEM changes into its true form as it does. A human stays human, and you've started a fight with a neighbour |
| 3 or more | Only THEM get here. IT gives up and the FBI takes it |

- **Someone has to take you seriously.** At least one listener must be none of these: bought, bitten, probed, one of
  THEM, or at 85+ suspicion of you. With no such listener, the ✓ still show but nothing happens. Nobody reacts, and
  that tells you the crowd belongs to THEM.
- Bystanders don't vote. They matter afterwards: they help in the fight (as today), and they're the ones who think
  you're mad when it fails.
- Being shaken changes nothing, but under Psychic 20 you can't confront at all.

## Suspicion: how weird people find you

The only thing people hold about you, and the reason to be careful.

| Level | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| 25+ | They remark as you pass: "You look a bit off today." |
| 60+ | They won't gossip with you or give you Biography facts, and chat works less often |
| 85+ | "Stay away from me." They don't count as a listener who takes you seriously |
| 3 ordinary people at 60+ at 06:00 | Mom calls and a letter comes from Wexley Clinic. Still three the next morning: you're committed, and the game is over |
| One of THEM at 60+ | THEY learn about you |

- **It goes up** when you gossip, snoop in front of them, put a camera in their face, have an accident in public,
  are seen peeing in the bushes, or fail a confrontation.
- **It comes down** when you chat, do a good day's work, or are proven right in public.
- **It shows in the evidence book only,** with a counter of how many people think you're mad ("2 of 3"). Not on the
  HUD.
- Nobody calls the police on you because of it.

## Psychic

It falls in a few big steps instead of a thousand small ones, and it never takes you all the way down by itself.

| What happens | Psychic falls to (if it's higher) |
| --- | --- |
| You see THEIR thing or a slight sign (a shed skin, THEIR device, a tell) | 70 |
| You see a true form | 50 |
| You fight one: you hit it, or it hits you | 30 |

Plus a steady drain while you're near it: fast near a true form or something wrong happening, slower near THEIR
things. The dozens of small hits it takes today go, and so does the maximum that shrinks while you stay low. The
dark at night stays, as a slow drift.

| Psychic | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Under 60 | Rare, slight visions: a shadow, someone running off, someone watching you who's gone when you look again. Rare trickery, not a mad world |
| Under 40 | Visions more often, and the world turns red at the edges. Your hands shake: no photos. You can still take things |
| Under 20 | No gossip and no confronting. You can still fight |
| 0 | A panic attack. You come out of it at 40 |

A vision never shows up on film (as today).

## Needs

Hunger and energy stay as they are. The bladder stays, for the funny moments and as one more thing to get wrong.
THEY slip in the restroom too, and a full bladder while you're tailing someone is good tension.

- **Drain:** 10 an hour awake, 2 an hour asleep, 15 per coffee. About three trips a day.
- **An accident in public:** everyone who sees it gets suspicious of you (as today). New: you smell until you
  shower, so people notice you from further away.
- **The bushes:** a quick pee in a hedge or the alley. Anyone who sees it gets suspicious, and while you're at it
  you're easy to notice, so not while you're tailing someone.

## Teaching it

- **Lenny** teaches each faction's signs, mixed with nonsense. He already does a little (`LENNY_FACTION`); keep it
  and extend it to the new Home, Habit and Biography tells.
- **The photo hint.** F shoots a wide wedge in front of you, with no aiming. When something worth a photo is in that
  wedge, the "F photo" hint at the bottom of the screen glows and a small camera mark appears over it. It glows for
  human secrets too, so it means "odd", not "THEM".
- **The card** after a photo says what you caught: "Priya · hugging the radiator. In October?"

## Open questions, and how they were settled

- Hale's filing cabinet as a Biography source: **not built**. Facts come from chat, rummaging at their place, the paper
  and Mom.
- "Come with me": **kept, without liking**. Anyone whose suspicion of you is under 25 comes along.
- Explained (grey), searched-and-nothing (dashed) and the CLEARED stamp: **notes for the player**. Seeing a secret
  explained or searching and finding nothing writes a note under the face; the automatic CLEARED stamp is gone, and
  your own CLEARED / IT'S THEM marks stay.
- The plants: **as in the tables**. Stolen cash from Pell's till for the lizards, a mark on the door frame for the dead.

## Building it

One pull request per step, each with a version bump and a new determinism baseline
(`python3 tools/regress.py --save tools/regress-baseline.json`). All six are in:

1. **Talking** (v1.11): Press out, gossip never makes cards, no daily limits, cards never spent, liking out, the
   suspicion counter in the book.
2. **Body and mind** (v1.12): the Psychic drops, drain and effects; the bladder numbers, the smell, the bushes.
3. **The confrontation** (v1.13) replaces the arena.
4. **Things** (v1.14): clear and fuzzy out, take-don't-photograph, the place rule, the Whose? tray and frame-ups out,
   the four kinds of hidden things, the fridge, THEIR plant, Destroy, protecting a victim, the signs for gut feeling.
5. **Cues** (v1.15): slipping off, the photo hint, slips you can hear, the flash rule, Lenny.
6. **Biography** (v1.16) replaces Contact.

A review pass (v1.17) made old saves start over and tidied the Biography page and the confrontation.

**Not built yet:** the rest of the Habit table (THEIR raw fish at lunch and taking a phone apart; the human
lookalikes: a cold by the radiator, a fisherman's sushi, Martin fixing the server rack, a raw-food diet, asleep on
their feet), and the smaller signs for a gut feeling (a new PC at home, walking in dazed).
